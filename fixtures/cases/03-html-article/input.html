Solid-State Batteries Reach Pilot Production

By Ana Kovács · July 18, 2026 · 4 min read

Two battery manufacturers said this week they have begun pilot-line production of solid-state cells, a milestone the industry has promised for a decade.

📬 Enjoying this story? Get TechWire in your inbox. Sign up free

The cells replace the liquid electrolyte with a ceramic separator, which eliminates the most common fire pathway and allows lithium-metal anodes with roughly 40% higher energy density.

What changes for EVs

Pilot volumes are small — thousands of cells per month — but automakers use these lines to validate manufacturing yield, the historical blocker. Yield above 90% would justify gigafactory retrofits.

  • Energy density: ~500 Wh/kg demonstrated vs ~350 Wh/kg for today's best NMC packs
  • Charge time: 10–80% in under 12 minutes on prototype packs
  • Cost: still 2–3× lithium-ion per kWh at pilot scale

The catch

Ceramic separators crack under thermal cycling, and both firms declined to publish cycle-life data beyond 800 cycles, well short of the 1,500 automakers require.
